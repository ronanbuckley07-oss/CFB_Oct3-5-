      Model check: actual plays vs. predictions

      Before each play the model predicts a full distribution of outcomes. When the play happens, it's scored against that prediction. One play says little because single-play yardage is heavily skewed. But by the central limit theorem, the sum of many play residuals (actual minus expected yards) is close to normal, with variance equal to the sum of each play's variance plus a small correlation term, since plays by the same team share that day's form (measured at ρ = 0.007 across NFL games since 2021, with no play-to-play momentum). That makes it possible to test whether a team is beating the model by more than chance.

      Backtest on finished games

      Replays real games through the model. At each sampled snap it simulates the rest of the game from that exact spot, then scores the win probability against who actually won, next to ESPN's own number from the same moment. Lower Brier score and log loss are better. Team strength comes from each game's closing betting line, or from the spread implied by ESPN's pregame win probability when ESPN kept no line, so this tests the in-game engine, not the pregame read. Each game is simulated once on the server and kept, so a game anyone has backtested before comes back instantly. Timeouts aren't in ESPN's play data, so every replayed snap assumes three each.

      What's going into this

      Player usage comes from ESPN box scores of each team's games this season. Targets on incompletions are read from the play description, so a few unlabeled throws are left out. Penalties are excluded, and injuries or depth chart changes since last week won't show up.

      Live mode fills all of this in from ESPN. Change anything and run it to test a spot by hand. That pauses the live feed until you tap Go live.
